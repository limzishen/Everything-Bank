# Sampler of Interview topics for C++/HFT candidates

Anonymous, May 2026


These ideas are just examples.

By no means is this document an attempt to be an exhaustive list of interview topics - it is not exhaustive at all.
Every interviewer tends to have their own question bank that they developed over the years; be prepared to handle personal preferences and quirks.   

For each topic, expect question difficulty to be modulated on the basis of candidate seniority and reported experience; standards for more junior candidates are calibrated lower, and vice versa.

## By Topic

- C++/Lambdas:
  - Study and understand how lambda captures work.
  - Be able to explain it from memory, at least the basics.
  - Homework: write an example in which a lambda is created that captures a variable by reference from an outside scope and is later invoked after the end of the scope's life. Investigate, understand and be ready to explain what happens at runtime as a result of the dangling reference.

- C++/Dynamic polymorphism:
  - Be able to offer a concise, correct definition of dynamic polymorphism and explain its intended purpose;
  - Homework: write a minimal polymorphic hierarchy (class Derived inheriting from class Base) in which the destructors were accidentally not declared virtual; be able to explain why that is undesired; make sure that your code triggers those undesired consequences as a demonstration. 
  - (Don't use AI except for grading your work when you are convinced you are done.) 
  - What happens if class `Derived` ’s constructor invokes a function declared as `virtual void prepare(void)` in `Base`, and overridden in `Derived`? Investigate and understand what's wrong with that.
  - Write minimal code to demonstrate the unintended consequences

- C++/Templates:
  - Be prepared to give (pen and paper) a minimal example of Class Template Argument Deduction (CTAD)
  - What is SFINAE? Be prepared to give (pen and paper) a minimal example.
  - Be prepared to explain variadic templates, pen and paper, to a colleague who does not know them
  - What's a template parameter pack?
  - In how many different grammatical contexts can you use an ellipsis (...)?
  - What is a fold expression? Why were they introduced?
  - Be prepared to give (pen and paper) a minimal example of fold expressions used in conjunction with variadic templates.
  - Homework: write sample code illustrating variadic templates, and show how you can make that code more concise via fold expressions.

- C++ / Miscellaneous features:
  - `consteval` - explain what it is the purpose of this keyword
  - What's the difference between `constexpr` and `consteval`?
  - `explicit` - explain what it is the purpose of this keyword
  - Be ready to write (pen and paper) a minimal block of code that has undesired consequences if explicit is not used, and those consequences are prevented by the use of explicit
  - (Do not use AI except to score/correct your answer when you are convinced you are correct.)
  - What is the point of keyword `override`?
  - Be ready to write (pen and paper) a minimal block of code performing the undesired action that `override` is intended to prevent.
  - Write, compile and execute that code. Verify that the undesired consequences occur at runtime.
  - (Do not use AI except to score/correct your answer when you are convinced you are correct.)
  - What is RAII?
  - What are structured bindings?

- C++ / Concurrency
  - Is `std::map` thread safe? If not, what are possible causes? What are some ways `std::map` could fail if used concurrently?
  - Homework 1: Write a multithreaded demonstrator that accesses std::map unsafely and manages to demonstrate undesired consequences at least “sort of” consistently. Don't use AI except for grading your work when you are convinced you are done.
  - Homework 2: Design a basic `thread_safe_map` class; do it from scratch, without AI help; Your class can be a minimal wrapper around `std::map`. Maybe rehearse till you are able to do it from first principles on paper with a pencil and no help. You don't need to get all the details right.
  - Write a multi-threaded performance benchmark to determine the throughput of your thread-safe map; what's the throughput? What is the bottleneck determining the throughput? 
  - Come up and evaluate ideas to optimize your class. 
  - Look at how the assembly output generated by the compiler changes as a result of your optimizations.
  - Verify the performance using your benchmark.

- Computer Architectures / Caches
  - Explain the cache hierarchy in a contemporary x86 server-grade CPU.
  - Be ready to offer reasonable ballpark estimates of the access latency in each cache level.
  - Homeworks: 
  - Write a microbenchmark that is capable of determining the size of your L1 cache (without asking the hardware). Explain how you did it. Verify it works.
  - Do not use AI except for grading your homework once you think it's completed.
  - Same for L2. Test on a different x86 CPU with a different L2 size.
  - Be ready to explain what's cache associativity
  - Write a microbenchmark that is capable of determining the associativity of L2.
  - In the processor you have in your PC, Is L2 shared among cores or private to each core?
  - Is L3? Write a multithreaded microbenchmark to prove that each level (L2 and L3) are private or shared.
  - Write a microbenchmark designed to measure the latency of L2. Pay extreme attention to the “hygiene” of your experimental conditions. How would you go about doing that?
  - Do the same for L3.
  - All the solutions and much more are in Ulrich Drepper, *What Every Programmer Should Know About Memory*. That book is worth its byte size in ounces of gold. It should be mandatory reading, and you should know it inside out.
  - Be ready to explain what's the MESI/MOESI protocol


- Computer Architectures / Atomics
  - Explain how modern hardware (multiple cores, multiple sockets, multiple levels of memory) implements efficiently atomic operations on a variable that is shared across threads running on cores located on different sockets;
  - Be able to explain what x86 instructions are involved
  - Be able to explain how std::atomic are implemented on x86
  - Homework: use godbolt's Compiler Explorer as needed to discover how std::atomic is implemented.

- Computer Architectures / Spinlocks
  - Be able to explain what's a spinlock
  - Homework: Write a spinlock demonstrator to communicate between two threads running on different cores.    
  - Write a spinlock-based ping-pong latency benchmark.
  - What's the x86 PAUSE instruction?

- Computer Architectures / False sharing
  - Be ready to explain what is false sharing.
  - Write a C++ demonstrator of false sharing.

- Algorithms / Complexity
  - Be ready to define what is an algorithm’s time and space asymptotic complexity
  - Be ready to determine the time complexity of most algorithms you could be asked to write on the whiteboard and justify your answers.
  - Homework: be ready to derive the time complexity of each operation on at least the most common standard C++ containers on the basis of the data structure implemented by each container. 

- Numerical representation:
  - Study and understand fp32 as specified in IEEE754. 
  - Be ready to explain from memory what's inside an fp32.
  - Be ready to derive and explain what is the largest positive finite number representable in an fp32.
  - Be ready to derive and explain what is the smallest positive number representable in an fp32.
  - What are denormals?
  - What is the minimum value for variable `float f` in C++ such that the following expression is true: 
`f +1.0f == 1.0f`? 
  - Derive this answer from first principles on pen and paper.
  - Write C or C++ code to verify you are correct.
  - Do not use AI except for grading your homework once you are sure it's completed. When using Google to search documentation, avoid or disregard the Gemini AI preview.

- Properties of fp32 addition
  - Question: I wrote a C++ program running oneapi::mkl::blas::...::gemm from the Intel MKL library, to multiply two matrices A and B. I'm computing C = A x B. I'm using gemm single-threaded. I'm running the same executable on server 1 with a CPU supporting AVX and server 2 with a CPU supporting AVX-512. I guarantee that matrix A on one server is bitwise identical to matrix A on the other server, and the same applies to B. However, I'm getting slightly different C outputs on server 1 vs 2. Why?
  - Investigate in depth. Write demonstrators. Pick them apart till you understand the answers.
  - Don't ask AI - the sooner you ask AI, the less you learn.


- Architectures / NUMA
  - Homework: write a microbenchmark that iteratively moves two threads on each pair of cores in a two-socket server and maps which cores are on the same socket and which cores are on different sockets. Explain your strategy.

## General knowledge

### Computer Architectures
Have a level of knowledge of computer architectures that matches your seniority/experience; e.g., if you have 5 years of x86 deep optimization experience, be ready to speak competently about it.

For fresh graduates, the standards are aligned with the knowledge offered at a college-level computer architecture course. You don't need to be as deep as a hardware designer, but you need to be familiar with memory hierarchies, ILP, DLP, TLP. 

If you didn't take Computer Architectures in college, this is a subject matter you can study at home. A good book to start with is the famous Hennessy and Patterson's Computer Architecture: A Quantitative Approach. It's a heavy book - you can't reasonably master all the contents. Make a reasonable selection of topics and attempt to gain an understanding of those in depth. 

### Operating Systems

Same considerations apply.

For fresh graduates, have a level of knowledge comparable with what is expected at a college OS course. Have a reasonable understanding of processes, memory management at the OS level, concurrency, IPC and synchronization, and filesystems.

Have a working knowledge of how to handle processes, threads, synchronization and IPC with the facilities offered by linux, possibly wrapped by C++

Consider a question like "describe what happens between the time you type ls <enter> on your terminal and the time you see the directory listing on your screen”.  Good performance is considered being able to talk for at least 15 minutes uninterrupted. The amount of topics and level of detail you can touch are immense. I've seen a candidate (strangely) familiar with keyboard input take 15 minutes just to describe what happens with keyboard input alone.

Extra credit: "describe what happens between the time you type ls <enter> on your terminal while your current directory is in an NFS mount, and the time you see the directory listing on your screen”.

One useful resource, although dated, is Samuel et al.'s *Advanced Linux Programming* https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/advanced-linux-programming/0735710430/
Plan at least to familiarize yourself with the core concepts there.

### Compilers
Unless you interview as a compiler specialist, you are not going to get deep questions on the algorithms used in the various stages of a compiler.
Still, understand the basic mechanism of software construction as it pertains to a C++: translation units, tokenization, parsing, ASTs, intermediate representations, instruction selection, optimization stages, static linking, dynamic linking, static and dynamic libraries, OS "interpreter", etc.
